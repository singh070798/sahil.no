VFX, Motion & 3D

Oskar Sylte is one of Norway's oldest soda producers, based in Molde. They have been a long-running retainer client at SDG\TBWA, and I've made social media content for them over several years. This case focuses on 2025–2026, when I delivered a steady stream of posts.

The challenge

The budget was limited, but the goal was always to deliver the best possible work within it. Motion performs better than stills on social media, so I pushed for animated posts wherever possible. The recurring ask was to place Oskar Sylte's products into different life style settings. And at times into setting that weren't possible to recreate because of the season, geography or availability at the time. Recreating the scenes digitally is relatively simple with a still images, but much harder in motion, and quick compositing tricks don't hold up once things start moving. Neither did AI-generated content as the brand's audience had reacted badly to earlier posts, calling it slop.

The approach

First, I built 3D models of all of Oskar Sylte's products, so we were no longer limited to existing pack shots. On top of that, I developed a workflow that lean into the strengths of AI-generation to streamline the project. I find stock images or build a plate with AI to act as the surrounding scene. Then integrate the 3D soda product into the scene. Animate the scene with AI in ComfyUI. Finally I reposition the labels back from the 3D assets into the final video so every logo, label and line of type on the product stays accurate. AI generation is a step in the process, not the finished product. The ideas, the product accuracy and the final craft stay in my hands.

Result

A steady flow of animated posts at a level the budget wouldn't otherwise allow. Oskar Sylte's dedicated audience has received the content well, without the "slop" comments that earlier AI-made posts attracted. I believe that's because the process leans on what AI does well and relies on manual work for the rest, rather than letting AI generate the whole result.
